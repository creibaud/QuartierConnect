title: Contracts and signing

When you provide or receive a paid service between neighbors, QuartierConnect creates a contract to formalise the exchange. This page explains where those contracts come from, how to review them, how to sign them securely and how to get hold of the PDF version.

## Where contracts come from

The **Contracts** page gathers every contract you have created and every contract you have to sign. A contract can show up in three ways:

- it is **generated automatically** as soon as a booking for a paid service is accepted;
- you **create it yourself**, naming one or more neighbors as signatories;
- you **import** an existing PDF document.

### The most common case: an accepted booking

Most contracts come from a paid service settled in points. Here is the full journey:

1. A neighbor publishes a paid service offer from the Services page.
2. You request a **booking** from the service page. The number of points is worked out from the service's rate.
3. The service owner finds your request in the **Received** tab of the Bookings page; you find it in the **Sent** tab.
4. When the owner **accepts** the booking, a **contract is generated automatically**. (While the request is still pending, they can also decline or cancel it.)
5. Both parties sign the contract. The points transfer is triggered **automatically as soon as the last signature is added**, sealing the exchange.

## Reviewing a contract

Every contract carries a **status** telling you where it stands:

| Status | What it means |
|--------|----------------------|
| Draft | The contract has been created but no signature has been added yet. |
| Partially signed | At least one party has signed, but signatures are still missing. |
| Fully signed | Every party has signed: the contract is final. |
| Cancelled | The contract is no longer active. |

These statuses always follow the same order: a contract starts as a draft, then moves forward as signatures are added, until it is fully signed. Cancelling remains possible for as long as it is not final.

From a contract's page, you can **view the PDF document** at any time. An **immutable audit log** comes with every contract: it records each event, in particular the document's SHA-256 hash and the signatures. This log cannot be altered, which guarantees the agreement's traceability.

## Signing a contract electronically

Signing happens in a guided dialog, in **three steps**:

1. **Read**: you go over the contract's terms and the number of points involved.
2. **Draw**: you draw your handwritten signature in the area provided.
3. **Strong validation**: you confirm by entering your **six-digit TOTP code**, generated by your authenticator app.

The three steps follow one another in order, and none can be skipped:

<Mermaid
  chart={`flowchart LR
  lecture["Step 1<br/>Read the terms"]
  trace["Step 2<br/>Draw your handwritten signature"]
  code["Step 3<br/>Confirm with a six-digit code"]
  lecture --> trace
  trace --> code`}
/>

> This last step is an important safeguard: your single-use code confirms that you really are the one signing.

Once **all signatories** have added their signature, the contract switches automatically to the **fully signed** status. The order of signatories does not matter: everyone signs whenever they want, and it is the **last signature** that seals the agreement.

Here is the full journey, from the creation of the contract to the final version kept for you:

<Mermaid
  chart={`flowchart LR
  genere(["Contract generated"])
  moi["I sign<br/>drawing + six-digit code"]
  autre["The other party signs<br/>in turn"]
  signe(["Contract fully signed"])
  archive["Viewable PDF<br/>timestamped audit log"]
  genere --> moi
  moi --> autre
  autre --> signe
  signe --> archive`}
/>

At every step, the event is written to the **audit log** with its timestamp: you keep a reliable, verifiable record of who signed, and when.

> As soon as that switch happens, the **points transfer is settled**. From then on, the matching
> booking **can no longer be cancelled**: the payment having gone through, the exchange
> is considered closed. Cancelling remains possible **for as long as the contract is not
> fully signed**.

## Downloading the PDF

A contract's PDF document can be **viewed** straight from its page, whatever its status. You go there to reread the agreement, check the terms or keep a copy.

## In short

- An accepted paid service **automatically** creates a contract between the two neighbors.
- The contract's status tells you whether signatures are still to be collected.
- Signing combines **your handwritten drawing** and **your TOTP code** for safe validation.
- A tamper-proof **audit log** and the **PDF document** stay viewable at any time.
