title: Report an incident

A streetlight out of order, illegal dumping on the street corner, a problem on the
public road? The **Incidents** page lets you report a problem in your neighborhood in
moments, then follow its handling through to resolution.

## Creating a report

Go to the **Incidents** page, then create a new report. Every report is made up of four
elements:

- **A title**: a short phrase summing up the problem (for example "Broken streetlight
  on Rue des Tilleuls").
- **A description**: the details needed to understand the situation.
- **A category**: picked from a list, it determines **who will see your report** (see
  below).
- **A location**: the exact spot concerned, which you choose directly **on a map**.

Once this information is filled in, your report is saved.

At a glance, here is the full journey, from your report to its resolution: you choose,
you locate, you describe, then you follow its progress.

<Mermaid
  chart={`flowchart LR
  debut(["A problem<br/>in the neighborhood"]) --> cat["Choose<br/>a category"]
  cat --> loc["Place a point<br/>on the map"]
  loc --> desc["Add a title<br/>and a description"]
  desc --> ouvert["Status:<br/>open"]
  ouvert --> encours["Status:<br/>in progress"]
  encours --> resolu["Status:<br/>resolved"]`}
/>

The first three steps are yours: together they make up the creation of the report. The
statuses then change without any action on your part (see below).

### Choosing the right category

The category is not just a label: it decides **who can see your report**. Three
categories are available:

| Category | What it is for | Who sees it |
|-----------|------------------|-------------|
| **Neighborhood issue** | A concrete problem in the public space: broken lighting, illegal dumping, pothole, etc. | The whole neighborhood |
| **Report (moderation)** | Content to bring to the moderators' attention (misplaced listing, inappropriate behavior, etc.). | You and the moderators |
| **Technical bug** | A malfunction in the application. | You and the moderators |

A **neighborhood issue** joins the public list and is visible to every resident, since
it concerns life in common.

> **Moderation** and **technical bug** reports stay **private**: they do **not** appear
> in the neighborhood's public list. Only you, their author, and the moderators have
> access to them. So you never see the moderation reports filed by other residents, and
> they do not see yours.

In other words, the category you choose directly decides who will have access to your
report:

<Mermaid
  chart={`flowchart TB
  sig["Your report"] --> choix{"Which<br/>category?"}
  choix -->|"Neighborhood issue"| tous["Visible to the<br/>whole neighborhood"]
  choix -->|"Report (moderation)"| prive["Visible to you<br/>and the moderators"]
  choix -->|"Technical bug"| prive`}
/>

When in doubt, remember the simple rule: a **neighborhood issue** is public, everything
else stays **private**.

### Choosing the right spot

The location is set by placing a point on the map. Take the time to position it as close
as possible to the problem: it helps the people who will follow up on the report find it
on the ground.

> If the point you choose sits **outside your neighborhood**, a warning appears. It is
> not a block: you can still save your report. It is simply an invitation to check the
> location.

## The life cycle of a report

Every incident has a **status** showing where its handling stands. That status changes
over time:

| Status | What it means |
|--------|----------------------|
| **Open** | The report has just been created and is waiting to be picked up. |
| **In progress** | The problem has been acknowledged and is being handled. |
| **Resolved** | The problem has been dealt with: the report is closed. |

You have nothing to do to move this status along yourself: it is the moderators of your
neighborhood who follow up on incidents and move each report from one state to the next
as it progresses.

Here is how a report travels from one status to another, and who triggers each step:

<Mermaid
  chart={`stateDiagram-v2
  state "Open" as ouvert
  state "In progress" as encours
  state "Resolved" as resolu
  [*] --> ouvert
  ouvert --> encours : a moderator picks it up

A report always follows this reading direction, from left to right: it does not go back
once resolved.

## Following progress

To find out the state of a report, open its **detail page**. It presents the incident
along with its **position** on the map, which lets you follow its progress at a glance.

In practice, an incident you report follows a simple journey:

1. You create the report from your application: it appears with the **open** status.
2. The neighborhood moderators pick it up and move it to **in progress**.
3. Once the problem has been handled on the ground, the report is marked **resolved**.

You can come back to the Incidents page at any time to browse the list of the
neighborhood's reports and check their status.

## In short

- A report is created with a **title**, a **description**, a **category** and a
  **location** picked on the map.
- The **category** decides visibility: a **neighborhood issue** is seen by everyone, a
  **moderation report** or a **technical bug** stays **private** (you and the
  moderators).
- A point placed outside your neighborhood triggers a **non-blocking warning**, not a
  refusal.
- Every incident goes through the **open**, **in progress** and **resolved** statuses in
  turn.
- The **detail page** lets you follow the incident and its position throughout its
  handling.
