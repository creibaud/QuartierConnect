The **Messages** page is your live messaging space with the residents of your
neighborhood. It lets you keep in touch, help each other out and organize
neighborhood life, all in one place. Here is how to use it day to day.

At a glance, here is the flow of a conversation, from the moment you pick a
neighbor to the messages going back and forth live:

<Mermaid
  chart={`flowchart LR
  demarrer(["Pick a neighbor<br/>by email address"]) --> conv["Open the<br/>conversation"]
  conv --> texte["Write<br/>a message"]
  conv --> photo["Attach a photo<br/>or a file"]
  conv --> vocal["Record a<br/>voice message"]
  texte --> direct(["Messages exchanged<br/>live"])
  photo --> direct
  vocal --> direct`}
/>

Every format (text, attachment, voice message) starts from the same conversation and
joins the same thread, which updates live for you and for your neighbor alike.

## Starting a conversation

To write to a neighbor, you start a new conversation. The search is done
**by email address**: enter the address of the person you want to contact,
then start the exchange.

Once the conversation is created, it stays available: you won't have to create it again
to keep talking to the same person later on.

> **Good to know:** remember to ask your neighbor for their email address so you can
> find them easily in the messaging space.

## Sending a message

Inside a conversation, you have several ways to express yourself:

- **Text messages** — write and send a message just like in any chat
  application.
- **Photos and files** — attach a photo or a file to your message, for example
  to share a picture or a useful document.
- **Voice messages** — record your voice and send it directly. The first
  time, the application will ask for **permission to access the microphone**; just
  accept it to be able to record.

These formats mix freely as the conversation goes on, depending on what you
feel like sharing.

When you send a text message, the application waits for a **delivery
confirmation**: your message is only considered sent once that confirmation
comes back.

> **If sending fails:** if something goes wrong (a dropped connection, for example), a
> message warns you that the text did not go out, and what you had typed **stays
> in the input box**. All you have to do is try again: nothing you write is
> lost silently.

In practice, a text message is only marked as sent once the confirmation
arrives; otherwise you are warned and can try again without having to retype your
text:

<Mermaid
  chart={`flowchart LR
  ecrire["You write<br/>and send"] --> attente{"Delivery<br/>confirmation?"}
  attente -->|Yes| ok(["Message sent"])
  attente -->|No| echec["You are warned<br/>the text stays in the input"]
  echec --> ecrire`}
/>

This only applies to **text messages**: it is that confirmation which
guarantees a message is never treated as sent until it has actually been
received.

## Following your conversations live

Messaging works **in real time**: new messages appear as they
come in, with no need to refresh the page.

Several indicators help you follow your conversations:

| Indicator | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Typing | Your neighbor is writing a message. |
| Unread messages | You have received messages you haven't read yet. |

These cues keep you from missing a reply and show you when the person you are
talking to is answering.

## Receiving and opening attachments

When a neighbor sends you a photo or a file, it appears directly in
the conversation. **Attachments can be downloaded from the conversation**:
you can therefore save them to your device to view them at any time.

## Finding your conversations again

All your conversations are gathered on the **Messages** page. There you'll find the
list of your conversations and can pick up an exchange where you left off.

Thanks to the **unread messages** indicator, you can spot at a glance the
conversations that contain new messages, so nothing goes unanswered.

In short, QuartierConnect messaging gives you a simple, friendly way to
stay connected with your neighbors: a quick note, a photo to share or a voice
message, each has its place in bringing your neighborhood to life.
