The map helps you take in your neighborhood at a glance: how far it stretches, the services offered around you and the places that bring your area to life. This page explains what the map shows and what you need to know about coverage.

## Your neighborhood on the map

As soon as you land on the dashboard, your home page, you'll find an **interactive map of the neighborhood**. It is built on the OpenStreetMap base map, the same kind of plan you often come across on the web, with the streets, squares and familiar landmarks of your town.

This map is centered on the neighborhood you are assigned to, the one your address connects you to. That makes it easy to find your bearings and spot the places you care about.

## The neighborhood boundary

Every neighborhood has a **boundary**, that is, a drawn zone marking out its territory. This outline is not decorative: it determines which neighborhood you belong to.

When you enter your address:

- If it falls **inside** the boundary of an existing neighborhood, your account is assigned to it automatically and you go straight to your dashboard.
- If it is recognised but **outside every neighborhood**, you are taken to a "waiting for coverage" screen.

The boundary also serves to **frame the maps**: it is what defines the portion of the plan highlighted for your neighborhood.

## Understanding coverage

**Coverage** means all the areas already carved into neighborhoods on the platform. That is what separates a place that is "covered" from one that is not yet.

- **Covered area**: your address falls inside an existing neighborhood. You then get access to all the local content, votes, events, services and incidents.
- **Uncovered area**: your address is recognised, but no neighborhood contains it. You then see the "waiting for coverage" screen.

> An uncovered area does not mean you have been forgotten. Your address is **saved** so that the administration team can extend coverage to your area. You can **correct your address** at any time if you think it was entered wrong.

Over time, new neighborhoods are created to take in the pending addresses: coverage therefore widens step by step.

## What the map shows

The map is not just a base plan. Depending on the page you are on, it locates your neighborhood's content:

| Page | What the map shows |
|------|------------------------|
| Dashboard | An interactive view of your neighborhood |
| Services | Service offers and requests that have a position set |
| Incidents | The location chosen to report a problem |
| Profile ("My neighborhood") | The neighborhood you are assigned to and its map |

On the **Services** page, only the services whose author has specified a position appear on the map. That way you can see in a moment who, near you, is offering a hand or looking for help.

> If a service's address cannot be located on the plan (address entered wrong or not found), the service is still published, but **without a point on the map**. So don't be surprised if a service you gave an address for doesn't always show up on the map: just correct the address and it will be placed again.

On the **Incidents** page, the map is used to **pick a point** to place your report (illegal dumping, for example). If the selected point is outside your neighborhood, a warning appears, without stopping you from continuing.

## In short

- The map places you in **your neighborhood**, on an OpenStreetMap background.
- The **boundary** delimits the neighborhood and decides which one you belong to.
- A **covered** area gives access to local content; an **uncovered** area puts your address on the list for future coverage.
- The map **locates** the services and incidents that have a position.
