title: Neighborhood votes

**Votes** are your neighborhood's consultations: a question put to every resident, which
everyone answers in their own way. This is where decisions get made together, with no
fuss — putting benches in the park, picking a date for the neighbors' party, splitting a
small budget between several ideas. This page explains how to take part and how to follow
the results.

In the application, you'll find these consultations on the **Community votes** page. An
overview of the **open votes** also appears on your dashboard, as soon as you land on the
platform.

## What a vote looks like

Every vote shows a **question**, sometimes a **description** to go with it, and an **end
date**. Under the title, the "Ends: …" line tells you how long you have to answer.

The page sorts consultations into three tabs, so you can find your way at a glance:

- **In progress**: votes still open, which you can take part in;
- **Answered**: the ones you have already responded to;
- **Closed**: consultations that are over, whose outcome you can review.

You can also sort the list by **ending soon** (the most urgent deadlines first) or by
**most recent**.

## The four ways to vote

Not every consultation is answered the same way. The vote type changes how you make your
choice, but the gesture stays just as simple: you tick, then you press **Vote**.

| Vote type | What you do |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Yes / No** | You settle a question one way or the other: only one answer possible. |
| **Single choice** | Several options are offered, you keep **just one**. |
| **Multiple choice** | You can select **as many options as you like**. |
| **Weighted vote** | You pick options and give each one an **importance** (a number), to say which ones matter most to you. |

A small visual cue helps you tell how many answers are expected: a **round dot** means a
single choice (one answer only), a **square box** means several answers are allowed.

For a **weighted vote**, as soon as you select an option, a small field appears next to
it: enter the weight you give it there. The higher the number, the more that option
counts toward the result.

> You vote **only once** per consultation, and your answer cannot be changed afterwards.
> Take the time to choose before you confirm.

## Your vote stays private

Some consultations are **anonymous**. In that case, the platform only ever shows you
**your own answer**: there is no way to know who voted for what. The results themselves
only show **totals** — the count of all answers put together, never naming anyone.

So you can take part with confidence: the neighborhood's general leaning is known, but
your individual choice is yours alone.

## Following the results live

As soon as you have voted, the consultation shows you the **live results** instead of the
ballot. Each option appears with its **progress bar**, its number of **votes** and its
percentage, and the number of **participants** is shown at the top. Your own answer is
highlighted with the **"Your choice"** label.

These results are alive: they move as your neighbors answer, for as long as the
consultation stays open.

## Open votes and closed votes

A consultation is **open** until its end date: you can answer it and follow the live
results. Past that deadline, it becomes **closed** — voting is no longer possible, and the
results shown become the **final results**. Whoever started the vote can also close it a
little earlier if needed.

If you didn't answer a vote in time and it is now closed, don't worry: you can still
review its outcome in the **Closed** tab.

## In short

- Find your neighborhood's consultations on the **Community votes** page, sorted into
  **In progress**, **Answered** and **Closed**.
- Depending on the vote type — **Yes / No**, **Single choice**, **Multiple choice** or
  **Weighted vote** — you keep one or several options, or even give them an importance.
- You vote **only once** per consultation, and your answer stays **private** on anonymous
  ballots.
- Once you have voted, follow the **live results** in real time; on the **end date**, they
  become the **final results**.

Your voice counts: browse the open votes regularly to weigh in on your neighborhood's
decisions.
